The measure of an investment's risk adjusted portfolio, calculated by comparing its return to that of a risk free asset

It can be used to determine if an investor's decisions are smart, lucky, or risky

A sharpe ratio above 1 is generally a good ratio.
### Pitfalls
Some portfolio managers will lengthen the portfolio to decrease the volatility. This is the case because annual returns are generally less volatile than monthly

The Sharpe Ratio calculates the volatility based on the [[Normal Distribution]]. This is often not the case with the stock market since there can be extremes
### Variations
- Sortino Ratio
- Treynor Ratio
### Formula
$$
\text{Sharpe Ratio} = \frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_p}
$$
$R_p$ = return of portfolio
$R_f$ = risk free rate
$\sigma_p$ = standard deviation of the portfolio's excess return
